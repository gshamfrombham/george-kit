## Why this matters
George's main goal now is a long-form theology teaching ministry on YouTube, in the spirit of Gavin Ortlund's Truth Unites: the Gospels and who Jesus is, why Scripture can be trusted, church history and the Reformation, and patient Protestant answers to Catholic and Orthodox claims. So this module measures him first as a theologian and teacher, and every theme it tracks is tied to the kind of long-form episode that would answer it (module 38) and the page that carries that episode's text. George already did the hard part. He disciples men every week, has taught church history for years, has walked with churches in Cuba, has written three books, and since September he has been publishing question-titled answers built for AI search. The baseline (an AI-visibility run on November 6, 2026: seven AI engines in English, US, plus three in Spanish, every answer read by hand) shows the engines are not using any of it yet: across 64 questions on his themes that don't use his name, he was named in 6 of 447 answers, and his Torchbearers series on Wycliffe, Hus, Tyndale and Luther was never cited. A fourth run the same day added 20 core theology questions (Protestant answers, Nicaea, the Trinity, covenant theology, the resurrection and more): 0 of 138 answers named him, while five of seven engines already list Truth Unites among the best Protestant theology channels. When people ask his name, the engines know him well and quote his site. When they ask the question he has already answered, they quote GotQuestions.org, Britannica, Wikipedia and YouTube instead. That gap is fixable, mostly by changing pages he already has, and the fixes fit inside the Tuesday video and Thursday article he already promised. This module turns the gap into a short list of page changes, a 30-day plan, and a monthly number per theme his agent checks, so George does not have to relearn any of it.
