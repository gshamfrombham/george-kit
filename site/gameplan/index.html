What his numbers say

  1. Long wins. Since 2024 his 60-minute-plus videos have the highest median views, 77,933. Videos of 20 to 30 minutes come next at 51,548.

  2. Charitable responses travel. Titles that respond to a bigger voice have a median of 51,603 views, against 37,590 for all his long-form.

  3. Shorts feed the channel. Clips cut from long videos bring a quarter of his views, 8.8 million.

What he does every week

  1. Scripts every word. “I script every word of every video.” Recording is the easy part.

  2. Keeps one day. One main video a week, mostly Monday mornings.

  3. Packages with care. His face on one side, 2 to 5 words that tease the title. He says he’s “definitely embarrassed” by his old thumbnails.

  4. Lands on the gospel. Every video, by design.

The question is the episode

Studies posted as-is get few views, even on huge channels. Series where each video answers one question do about double.

  • Ligonier sermons titled by passage, on a channel of 1.65 million8,000 to 12,000
  • Ortlund’s sermon and devotional videos, median18,426
  • All his long-form, median37,590
  • His Genesis series, one question per video, median77,576

So every study becomes one episode titled by its question: “Does God Hear Unanswered Prayer? (James 5)”. Source: public YouTube data, November 6, 2026.

Film the study, edit the series

  1. Plan a 6 to 10 week study series. Each session answers one question, worded the way people ask it.

  2. Film each study. Module 38 covers the setup and consent from the room.

  3. Your Claude cuts each study into one 25 to 50 minute episode with captions, scripture on screen and chapters, using HyperFrames (module 39).

  4. You watch the cut and say “good” or list changes. Nothing goes up raw.

Thursday articles become essays

Your article is already a script. About once a month during a series, one becomes a 15 to 30 minute scripted essay. Between series, essays carry the channel.

Every essay opens the way his best ones do:

  1. A question or a news peg
  2. One line on what the video will do
  3. Honest credit to the other side
  4. A numbered roadmap
  5. The landing: the gospel

Titles ask, thumbnails tease

  • The question first in the title, 6 to 10 words
  • 2 to 4 large words that add to the title, never repeat it
  • Your face on one side, one word in the accent colour
  • The series badge in the same corner every time
  • Three clearly different versions; YouTube’s Test & compare keeps the one people watch longest (module 40)
